## Hierarchy decisions

**Rule (Scott).** Journals describe the same higher-order category at different levels of detail: some name only the whole, others name its parts. A reports-only group or a journal construct becomes a subcategory of a higher-order category when it is a narrower case of the same type of error. Close-but-overlapping pairs noted above are fine as they stand.

| # | Decision | Reason |
|--:|:--|:--|
| H1 | New higher-order category **Methods** (label from the journals: JIBS "Methods", AMJ "Methodological rigor", AMLE "Methodology"). Criteria naming methods as a whole attach to Methods itself. | Design, rigor, data, and methodology are all the same thing; journals differ only in how many parts they name |
| H2 | Subcategories of Methods: Research design and data; Analysis and statistical methods; Measurement; Robustness; Sample selection; Alternative explanation not ruled out (with its sub-dimension "Causal claim rests on a measured, not manipulated, variable"); Analysis plan or model not specified | Each is a part of the study's methods, from the journals or the reports |
| H3 | New higher-order category **The research question**, with subcategories Importance of the problem (why it matters) and Purpose of the research (what it sets out to do) | "Purpose unclear" is not a narrower case of "importance not argued"; both are parts of the research question, which journals name at different levels (ASR "Research questions and goals"; AJPS "Importance of problem") |
| H4 | New higher-order category **Interpretation of results** (Organization Science row "Analysis and Interpretation"), with subcategories Conclusions supported by the evidence and Finding left undeveloped | An undeveloped finding is not a narrower case of an unsupported conclusion: one claims more than the results show, the other less. Both concern interpreting results |
| H5 | Selective or misleading presentation of results becomes a subcategory of Conclusions supported by the evidence, under Interpretation of results | A narrower case of the same error: presenting results selectively claims more than the evidence shows (ASQ "Presentation of evidence" is already in that category) |
| H6 | Extension suggested becomes a subcategory of Overall contribution to knowledge | A narrower case of the same error: the paper adds less to knowledge than it could, and the critique names a specific way to add more |
| H7 | The two "internal inconsistency" critiques (R125-C02, R289-C12) go to Writing and presentation; the reports-only category "Internal inconsistency" is retired | They are numbers that differ between passages, an error in the text. Scholars mean something else by internal inconsistency: the theory says X causes Y, but the test or discussion says Y causes X, or only that X and Y are correlated. That meaning is covered by the journal construct Coherence of the argument (ASR: "internal contradictions or inconsistencies in the author's theoretical argument") |
| H8 | New higher-order category **Theory**. Subcategories: Theory development (the report group "Theory weak" matches it); Coherence of the argument, with its own subcategory Internal contradictions (ASR wording); Fit between theory and analysis; Falsifiability | Each is a part of the paper's theory, from the journals or the reports, which journals name at different levels (Organization Science "Development of Theory"; ASR "Coherence of conceptual argument" and "Linkage of argument and analysis") |
| H9 | Breadth of interest becomes a subcategory of Novelty, originality, or interestingness | A narrower case of the same idea: interestingness measured by how many readers find the paper interesting |
| H10 | Length relative to contribution becomes a subcategory of Overall contribution to knowledge | The journals judge length against contribution ("contribution relative to their length") |
| H11 | Relevance and fit to the journal stays top-level | It asks whether the paper belongs in this journal; a novel paper can still be off-topic |

## Name-equivalence decisions

**Rule (Scott).** The question is not how critiques are sorted but whether a journal name and a report name refer to the same thing in reviewers' eyes. Journals-only categories are compared, one at a time, with the closest report group.

| # | Journal name | Report name | Same thing? |
|--:|:--|:--|:--|
| N1 | Length relative to contribution (Management Science; ASQ "value per page"; Econometrica "conciseness") | Organization, length, and placement | Yes |

**Rule (Scott).** When a journal category and a report group are the same thing, the journal version is the source of truth for both the label and the placement.

| N1 placement | Length relative to contribution stays under Overall contribution to knowledge, and the report group Organization, length, and placement (13) moves there from Writing and presentation | Journal version is the source of truth |

## Inclusion rule, revised (Scott)

A dimension counts if it appears in referee reports, on a journal's review form, or in the scholarly literature. The literature is added because the pilot's reports come mostly from venues that do not judge theory, so theory dimensions come from scholars and empirical ones from reports. Order: the literature is folded into the list first, then the remaining reports are coded against the complete list, batch by batch, under the stopping rule.

## Literature decisions

Most literature criteria (step5_skeleton.md, Version A) match existing dimensions. Seven do not and are decided one at a time.

| # | Literature criterion (sources) | Decision |
|--:|:--|:--|
| L1 | External validity (Campbell & Stanley 1963; Shadish, Cook & Campbell 2002; Deaton & Cartwright 2018; Guba 1981) | Sub-dimension of Scope, implications, and limitations: scope is generalizability, which is external validity |
| L2 | Replicability (Open Science Collaboration 2015; Simmons, Nelson & Simonsohn 2011; Guba 1981 "dependability") | Sub-dimension of Legitimacy of Conclusions: a finding that may not replicate may not be real, so the conclusion is not secure. Not Incomplete account |
| L3 | Neutrality (Guba 1981 "confirmability"; Tracy 2010 "sincerity"; Ioannidis 2005 "financial and other interests") | Same thing as the report sub-dimension "Selective or misleading presentation of results", which takes the literature label **Neutrality** |

**Label rule (Scott).** Use the journals' label first; if no journal offers one, use the literature's label; use a Claude-written label only if neither offers one.
| L4 | Credibility of qualitative accounts (Guba 1981; Tracy 2010) | Same thing as Alternative explanation not ruled out (internal validity; Guba calls credibility its naturalistic analog). Label to follow the label rule: AMJ's form uses "internal validity" |
| L5 | Grounding of theory in data (Gioia, Corley & Hamilton 2013; Eisenhardt 1989; Tracy 2010 "rich rigor") | Same thing as Fit between theory and analysis, the inductive version of it. Label per the rule: ASR's heading "Linkage between Conceptual Argument and Empirical Analysis" |
| L6 | Research ethics (Tracy 2010) | Same thing as the two "ethics approval or consent not reported" critiques; they become a sub-dimension of Incomplete account of process or findings under the literature label **Research ethics** |
| L7 | Explanatory power and utility of theory (Kuhn 1977; Bacharach 1989 "utility"; Sutton & Staw 1995; Corley & Gioia 2011 "scientific utility") | A new sub-dimension of Development of Theory. How much a theory explains and predicts is a different statement from the theory being thin or narrow, or from whether the work tests, creates, or extends theory; these are separate sub-dimensions of theory |
