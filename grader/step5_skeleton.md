# Step 5. Deductive skeleton, built two ways

Provenance: every entry in this document traces to `step1_scholarly_literature.md` (criteria from 24 retrieved scholarly works) or `step2_journal_forms.md` (criteria from 16 journal entries: 15 journals plus the APA's association-level guidance), and those files trace to texts retrieved on 2026-09-22.
No criterion, definition, or page number has been added from memory; where an input entry has no page number, the gap is carried forward here.
The skeleton is built two ways, a broad Version A and a narrow Version B, and the choice between them is open for the authors.

## 1. Sort of every criterion (Step 4 test)

Every criterion in both input files was sorted by the protocol's Step 4 test alone:

> A rigor criterion or critique concerns an error that a reader can show to be true or false by checking the manuscript's text, tables, or data, a cited source, or a stated logical or statistical rule. A contribution criterion or critique concerns a judgment about the value of the manuscript that no such check can show to be false.

The source of a criterion and the label the input file gives it played no part in the sort.
Because the test defines rigor by the availability of a check, a criterion that names no check is placed on the contribution side.
Where the sort departs from the input file's label, or the criterion fits the test awkwardly, it is listed in Section 2.

Totals: 283 criteria were sorted (194 from Step 1 and 89 from Step 2).
Of these, 169 sort as rigor (144 from Step 1, 25 from Step 2) and 114 as contribution (50 from Step 1, 64 from Step 2).
The sort departs from the input label for 50 criteria, and 73 criteria in all are listed as hard cases.

## 2. Hard cases

This section lists every criterion whose sort departs from the input file's label and every criterion that fits the test awkwardly.
Each was assigned by the literal test; no third category was used.
Two general points apply throughout.
First, the test sorts critiques, while many input rows are instructions, verdicts, or findings rather than standards for a manuscript; these were sorted by whether the row names a check, and those that name none fall on the contribution side.
Second, no input criterion concerns data availability as such; the nearest are the disclosure requirements of Simmons, Nelson and Simonsohn (2011) and the registration criterion of Nosek et al. (2018), both sorted as rigor.

### 5.3 Version B categories that no Version A criterion supports

Every Version B category has at least one matching Version A criterion, but two are weakly supported.
- **Incorporation of Relevant Literature** has no support on its own side. The Version A criterion that matches it (R18, 4 sources) sorts as rigor under the Step 4 test; the only contribution-side match is part of one AJPS row.
- **Relevance to Organization Science** is supported only by generic journal-fit criteria from two sources (AMD and the APA journals) and, in part, by breadth of interest. No input source concerns organization science as such, and the Step 2 search found no public Organization Science reviewer criteria.

In addition, statistical conclusion validity is supported by four Version A sources, but its own definition is not in the retrieved portion of Shadish, Cook and Campbell (2002).

### 5.4 The three most important gaps

1. **Version B's contribution side has no row for originality, interestingness, or the overall significance of the contribution.** These are among the most widely supported contribution criteria in Version A: originality and novelty (C2) has 7 sources, significance of the contribution (C6) has 12, and interestingness (C1) has 4, including all fifteen rows from Davis (1971). Under Version B, a critique that a paper is not new or not interesting has no row to fall in.
2. **Version B's rigor side omits most of the transparency and credibility criteria and the qualitative criteria.** Falsifiability, replicability, neutrality, grounding of theory in qualitative data, and candor about limitations have no category, and analytic flexibility, preregistration, and robustness fit only partly under statistical conclusion validity. These criteria come from Popper, Ioannidis, Simmons and colleagues, Nosek and colleagues, the Open Science Collaboration, Leamer, Guba, Tracy, Gioia and colleagues, and Eisenhardt, and from the AER, ASR, Psychological Science, and AMPPS forms.
3. **The two versions put some criteria on opposite sides of the rigor-contribution line.** Version B treats literature incorporation and theory development as contribution, but under the Step 4 test the critiques that literature is missing (R18) and that a theory gives no explanation of why (R3, drawn from Sutton and Staw, Whetten, and Bacharach) are rigor. Because the study's comparison turns on this line, the authors' choice of version changes how such critiques would be coded. Separately, clarity of presentation (C9, 7 sources) has no place on either side of Version B.
