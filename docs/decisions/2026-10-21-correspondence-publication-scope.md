# Protect the substance of the sample's correspondence; allow counts and processing metadata

**Decided:** 2026-10-21 (David, clarifying the publication rule after the independent repository review).

This supersedes the blanket prohibition on publishing anything derived from the sample's correspondence in `2026-10-21-journal-correspondence-private.md`.

- The sample article's referee reports and decision letter remain private. Do not publish their text, quotations, paraphrases, summaries, or material that lets a reader reconstruct the concerns, arguments, or judgments expressed in them.
- Counts of reports and critiques, including counts per report or letter, may be published. Processing metadata may also be published: word and token counts, model calls, failures and retries, cost, and elapsed time. Combined totals and counts obtained by subtraction are allowed. These describe the amount of correspondence or its processing without revealing its substance.
- Critique-level codes and manuscript locations remain withheld. Category and dimension distributions, rigor or contribution shares, and comparisons describing what the referees or editor criticized also remain withheld: these convey substantive meaning about the correspondence even without quoting it. They stay in the submitted technical appendix.
- The proposal and assembled technical appendix remain submitted rather than published. This clarification does not change how the editor's decision is handled; the earlier decision records it as the authors' own outcome to disclose.
- `ref publish` continues to omit `confidential/`, the proposal, the assembled appendix, and the journal arms' coded rows and review-specific manifest entries. It may retain counts, run narratives limited to processing facts, and combined processing totals. The existing exclusions remain safeguards; allowing metadata does not require publishing every available field.
- This applies to the sample only. The study's coded dataset is governed by the journal's data agreement and the Stage 3 publication plan.

**Why:** David's concern is disclosure of substantive correspondence that a reader could interpret, rather than the mere existence, length, or critique count of a report or letter. A critique count alone does not establish what was criticized, its severity or validity, or the editorial outcome.
