# The sample's referee reports and decision letter appear only in the submitted appendix

**Decided:** 2026-10-21 (David and Scott, in conversation).

**Publication scope superseded:** David clarified later the same day that counts and processing metadata may be published; the substance of the correspondence remains private. See `2026-10-21-correspondence-publication-scope.md`. The original decision below is retained as a record; its blanket prohibition on derived numbers no longer applies.

- The sample article's referee reports and decision letter are private communications between Organization Science, the authors, and the referees. Nothing drawn from them is published: not their text, not their coded critiques, and not the numbers or comparisons computed from them.
- They are reported in coded form in the technical appendix submitted with the proposal. The appendix is therefore submitted, not published, and so is the proposal (David, the same day), whose source states the editor's decision. The decision itself is the authors' own outcome to disclose (David, the same day), but the public appendix template states neither the outcome nor the number of reports.
- The working repository keeps everything. `ref publish` leaves out folders named `confidential`, the generated appendix, and the proposal, removes the referee reports' and the letter's rows from the grader runs' coded critiques and manifests, and refuses if any remain. `ref numbers` writes a public version of the prototype numbers covering the AI reviews only, and the full version to `sample/confidential/`.
- The published repository also adds the PCI Organization Studies pilot's codes and tally script, which the rules-agreement test cites, as with the BMJ Open pilot.
- The repository says where the material went: the public README, `sample/README.md`, `sample/manuscript/README.md`, and the public numbers file.

- **Scope (David, the same day).** This covers the sample article, whose referees and editor never agreed to any use of what they wrote. It does not govern the study: the critique-level coded dataset of the journal's reviews that the proposal describes is published at Stage 3 as the proposal says, under the data agreement with the journal.
- The technical appendix lists the sample's coded critiques row by row (codes, positions, locations, and word counts, no text), so the editors can recompute every number from the codes, as the proposal promises for the study.

**Why:** the authors' consent covers the manuscript, not the correspondence; the referees and the editor have not agreed to publication of anything drawn from what they wrote.
